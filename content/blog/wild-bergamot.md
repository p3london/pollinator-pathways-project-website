Wild Bergamot (Bee Balm) is a vibrant perennial native to North America known for its bold colours, fragrant blooms, and adaptability in gardens. This easy-to-grow plant, part of the mint family, attracts pollinators while adding visual appeal to pollinator gardens, herb gardens, and containers. Wild Bergamot is native to most provinces and territories across Canada. Its spreading habit can be managed by regular division, helping to maintain its size and provide new plants for friends and family. As a low-maintenance choice, Wild Bergamot (Bee Balm) thrives in various landscapes, offering culinary and medicinal benefits along with ecological value.

## Wild Bergamot (Bee Balm) Care

Wild Bergamot (Bee Balm) is a low-maintenance, native plant that thrives with very little intervention once established. Here are the key things to know about growing Wild Bergamot (Bee Balm) in your garden or landscape.

- Choose a bright location that receives several hours of direct sunlight for the best show of blooms.
- Plant in a rich, well-draining potting medium and avoid overly sandy or clay soils.
- Keep the soil evenly moist, but avoid [overwatering](https://www.thespruce.com/signs-of-overwatering-houseplants-8647868).
- Lightly mulch newly planted Wild Bergamot (Bee Balm) with natural mulch or compost but do not fertilize. Over-fertilization can commonly cause Wild Bergamot (Bee Balm) to stop producing flowers.

### How to Grow Bee Balm From Seed

Bee balm grows readily from seed, whether you want to sow the seeds directly or start them indoors. Direct sow bee balm seeds in the garden a few weeks before the first fall frost for a simple, low-maintenance approach. Seedlings should sprout in the spring without any further intervention or care!.

If you want to start bee balm seeds indoors, you can do so in early spring, about four to six weeks before the last frost in your area. However, be aware that bee balm seeds require a period of cold stratification to sprout.

Then, follow these instructions to grow and care for bee balm seedlings indoors.

1. Sow seeds in a loose, loamy potting mix pre-moistened in seed trays or small containers.
2. Place the containers in a warm, bright location and keep the soil evenly moist.
3. Once the seeds have sprouted, begin [hardening them off](https://www.thespruce.com/how-to-harden-off-plants-1402554) to outdoor conditions (weather permitting).
4. Transplant outdoors once the last frost date in your area has passed and the seedlings fully acclimate to outdoor conditions.

### Light

Bee balm grows best in a location that receives several hours of bright, [direct sunlight](https://www.thespruce.com/what-is-full-sun-partial-shade-1402372) each day. It can also grow in partially shaded locations, although its flowering will be less prolific.

### Soil

Bee balm grows naturally in woodland areas, so it prefers moist, rich, and well-drained soils. Avoid planting this flowering perennial in heavy clay or sandy soils; instead, choose loamy, well-drained locations.

### Water

Like most herbs, bee balm thrives in consistently moist soil. However, this hardy plant can withstand brief periods of drought as long as the soil is not left to dry out for too long.

### Temperature and Humidity

This perennial flower is native to North America and hardy in [USDA zones 3 to 9](https://www.thespruce.com/find-your-usda-zone-3269819). It prefers dry conditions and does not require humidity to grow.

In fact, too much moisture can cause issues with the plant, making it more susceptible to diseases like rust and powdery mildew. Bee balm can handle a range of temperatures and easily withstands freezing winters.

### Fertilizer

Bee balm enjoys nutrient-rich conditions, but as long as it is planted in soil rich in organic matter, it does not require supplemental fertilization. In fact, over-fertilization can cause the plant to stop blooming, as it focuses on foliage production instead of flowers.

## Pruning

Bee balm does not require aggressive pruning, but regular maintenance throughout the year can benefit the plant's health and appearance. For example, deadheading spent flowers throughout the season encourages more prolific blooms.

Pinching back the stem tips in the early spring can also help create a bushier appearance. [Cut the plant back in the fall](https://www.thespruce.com/when-to-cut-back-bee-balm-11822028) to a few inches above ground level to help encourage new growth in the spring.

## Propagating Bee Balm

Like many plants in the [mint family](https://www.thespruce.com/growing-mint-1402628), bee balm has a spreading growth habit, making it ideal for propagation by division. This is the simplest method for propagating this flowering perennial, although it can also be easily grown from seed.

Bee balm can also be grown from cuttings, although this method is less popular because it can be much slower.

Propagation by division and seeds is best done in the spring. Avoid dividing bee balm too late in the summer or fall, as the plant won't have time to establish before its winter dormancy. Cuttings, on the other hand, should be taken in the mid-to-late summer for the best chance of success.

To divide bee balm, follow these steps.

1. Dig up a clump of bee balm using a shovel or narrow spade. The crown, roots, and [rhizomes](https://www.thespruce.com/rhizomes-2131103) should all come together in a large cluster.
2. Using a sharp trowel, divide the bee balm into two or more clumps, depending on the size.
3. Freshly divided bee balm is ready for transplanting immediately or can be kept in containers filled with soil for later planting.
4. Water the newly transplanted bee balm thoroughly.

To propagate bee balm by cuttings, follow these steps.

1. Use a pair of sharp, clean pruning shears or scissors to take softwood stem cuttings from a healthy plant. The cuttings should be 3 to 4 inches tall.
2. Remove the bottom leaves from each cutting.
3. Prepare seed trays or small containers with a pre-moistened, loamy potting mix and plant the cuttings in the soil. [Rooting hormone](https://www.thespruce.com/how-to-use-rooting-hormone-1902934) is optional.
4. Water the cuttings regularly to keep the soil moist, but avoid overwatering. The soil should be damp but never soaking.
5. Keep the trays or containers indoors or in a cold frame outdoors for the winter. Plants will be ready for transplanting the following growing season but may take a couple of years to flower.

[Soil amendments](https://www.thespruce.com/guide-to-soil-amendments-7095754) can help increase the soil quality in your garden if you think it needs a boost. Light mulching with organic mulch or compost is another great way to give your plant a natural boost.

## Potting and Repotting

Many bee balm cultivars are popular as container plants, whether in raised beds or pots. However, since bee balm has a spreading growth habit, repotting may be required after one to two years in the same container.

To repot bee balm, choose a container four to six inches larger than the previous container. Fill the excess space with plenty of fresh, loamy potting soil and water well after planting. Repotting is best done in the early spring months when the plant is just coming out of dormancy.

## Overwintering

Bee balm planted in the garden requires no overwintering during harsh winter temperatures. However, plants growing in containers may need some help during extreme winter temperatures since their roots aren't as well insulated. Consider moving them into a covered outdoor greenhouse or providing winter cover until temperatures begin warming in the spring.

## Common Pests and Diseases

[Powdery mildew](https://www.thespruce.com/powdery-mildew-1402500) is by far the most prevalent issue to watch for when growing bee balm. Nearly all varieties of bee balm are susceptible to this common disease, although some cultivars are known for being more resistant than others. Watch your plants for signs of powdery mildew and treat early infections with natural fungicides.

As for pests, common garden pests like spider mites, [aphids](https://www.thespruce.com/how-to-get-rid-of-aphids-naturally-8645106), [thrips](https://www.thespruce.com/take-control-of-thrips-2656317), and stalk borers can all afflict bee balm. However, the plant is not known for being especially prone to any of them. Early prevention and management isareey to preventing and treating any pest infestation.

## How to Get Bee Balm to Bloom

Bee balm is known for its showy, bright flowers that attract a variety of pollinators including bees, hummingbirds, and butterflies. They require no intervention to encourage blooming, although [regular deadheading](https://www.thespruce.com/deadheading-flowers-8651358) can help the plant produce more. If your bee balm plant is not blooming, this likely indicates an underlying issue with your plant, which requires further investigation.

## Common Problems With Bee Balm

Native to North America, bee balm is known for being low-maintenance and requiring very little intervention in the garden. However, they are not exempt from the occasional problem. Besides powdery mildew, a lack of blooms is one of the most common problems gardeners encounter.

### Absence of Blooms

Bee balm is known for being a prolific bloomer, so an absence of blooms is usually noticed quickly. If your bee balm is not blooming, it's likely for one of a few reasons.

First, the plant may not be getting enough light. These perennials require plenty of bright, direct sunlight (sun beams hitting the leaves) to produce flowers. If light isn't the issue, your plant may be experiencing poor soil conditions, over-fertilization, or overcrowding, all of which can affect blooming.
