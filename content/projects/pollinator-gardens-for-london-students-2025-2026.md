With the assistance of funding from the City of London’s Grassroot Grant (administered by the London Environmental Network), and the Jane Goodall Canada foundation’s A.P.E. grant, Pollinator Pathways Project (P3) volunteers conducted 7 workshops at 4 TVDSB elementary schools. The 4 schools, and their teacher coordinators, are:

- Rick Hansen PS - Steve Cassidy coordinator – Grade 5/6
- Arthur Ford PS – Alexandra Arcese and Alisa Murphey Smith coordinators – Grade 3
- Louise Arbour FIPS – Angela Efstratudakis coordinator – Grade 8
- Sir Isaac Brock PS – Averil Novak and Jenny Harper coordinators – Grade 2/3

Through a series of 7 workshops with the 4 classes at the participating schools by P3 volunteers, students gained practical knowledge and experience by growing native perennial plants from seed and collaboratively creating a pollinator garden in their schoolyards. The funding for the program provided the equipment, materials and expertise needed to educate the students on planting pollinator gardens for the local environment. The equipment provided to the schools, and the workshop instructions, will allow the coordinators at each school to conduct similar projects in future years, growing the size of their pollinator gardens and continuing to provide hands-on educational opportunities for future students

The 7 workshops were conducted over the school year as follows:

- Late Sept/25 - a presentation on how the pollination process improves local biodiversity
- Early Oct/25 - preparation of the pollinator garden site - black plastic and wooden surround
- Late Oct/25 - Direct Sowing method - seeds in soil cubes prepared by students
- Early Nov/25 - students planting the soil cubes prepared using the Direct Sowing method
- Early Dec/25 - Overwinter Seedling method
- Late Mar/26 - Spring Seedling method
- Late May/26 - planting Overwinter and Spring seedlings

In order to measure the impact of the project, several of the teacher coordinators sent in the following testimonials:

## Testimonials

### Steve Cassidy, Grade 5/6 Homeroom, Rick Hansen P.S.

> The Pollinator Gardens Project was a clear success in both student engagement and learning outcomes. The impact of the project was measured through qualitative feedback and ongoing conversations with students, who consistently expressed excitement and appreciation for the opportunity to engage in hands-on environmental learning, particularly in relation to biodiversity and ecosystem connections. Students were highly engaged throughout the pollinator garden project, which provided a rich, experiential learning opportunity for my class and is developing into a lasting space that will benefit the entire school community.
>
> The project supported students in learning key concepts related to plant life cycles, including germination, seed starting, direct sowing, greenhouse propagation, and winter sowing techniques. Students also developed a deeper understanding of the ecological importance of native grasses, including carbon sequestration, root interactions with soil minerals, and their role in supporting insect populations. Through practical application, students planted seeds, tracked germination success rates across varying conditions, and observed early plant growth in a classroom-built greenhouse. Daily care tasks such as watering fostered responsibility and stewardship, while family volunteers extended this learning into the summer months through ongoing maintenance support.
>
> Once established, the garden will be enjoyed by approximately 300 students and staff, providing both educational and aesthetic value. Overall, this project has had a meaningful impact on student learning and school culture and demonstrates strong potential for continued success with sustained funding.

### Alexandra Arcese, Learning Support Teacher/Teacher Librarian/CST, Arthur Ford P.S.

> We were able to measure the impact of the Pollinator Project at our school through the observations and conversations that were ongoing with our Grade 3 students.
>
> Before beginning the project, students were invested in the presentation that introduced the various species of plants that they would be learning about. They asked very thoughtful questions during the presentation and were able to relate the discussion to curriculum that had been taught in class. Part of the Grade 3 Science curriculum is a study on plants. This project naturally integrated into classroom learning and even inspired a classroom STEM project in which students built structures to create green roofs in urban communities.
>
> The impact of this project could be seen from the excitement shown by students when they had the opportunity to care for the Spring seedlings that were kept in a greenhouse in the school. It was seen from the care and intention students had when clearing garden spaces and discussing how they would plant and distribute the seedlings they had worked with in the winter.
>
> Our students were actively engaged in this project which helped to create meaningful learning opportunities. Their knowledge of pollinators and the impact of pollinator gardens vastly increased.

## Results Achieved

- Number of students participating – there were 108 students participating in the workshops at the 4 schools
- Number of seedlings grown – there were 237 seedlings grown to a level that they could be planted in the 4 gardens, with 104 grown using the Overwinter Seedling method and 135 using the Spring Seedling method. For the Direct Sow method, we’ll have to wait until the spring of 2027 to see their success rate as the direct sow seeds don’t typically develop into plants until the second season.
